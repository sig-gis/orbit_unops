3. Urban Expansion Analysis

The flagship SDG 11.3.1 feature. Click Urban Expansion in the sidebar to open the analysis panel. If you are on a different view (e.g. Jobs), it will automatically switch to the Map View first, then open the panel.

What You See

  1. Bar Chart — Urban area (km²) for each year from 2018 to present
  2. Baseline Selector — Choose which year to use as the reference baseline
  3. Summary Stats — Baseline value, Current value, CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)
  4. Year Buttons — Click to overlay that year's urban mask on the map

Using Year Overlays

  1. Click a year button (e.g. 2020) in the panel
  2. The platform fetches the urban extent tile for that year from Earth Engine
  3. The overlay appears on the map with a year-specific colour
  4. Click multiple years to compare — overlays stack
  5. Click an active year button again to remove its overlay

SDG 11.3.1 — LCRPGR Indicator

Below the bar chart, the platform displays the official UN-Habitat SDG 11.3.1 indicator — the Ratio of Land Consumption Rate to Population Growth Rate (LCRPGR).

Formula: 
LCR = ln(U_current / U_baseline) / n
PGR = ln(Pop_current / Pop_baseline) / n
LCRPGR = LCR / PGR

4. Creating & Managing Jobs

Job Submission Wizard

Click + New Job in the header. The 4-step wizard guides you through:

  1. Step 1 — AOI: Select a pre-defined area, or click "Draw New AOI on Map" to draw a rectangle by clicking two opposite corners on the map
  2. Step 2 — Analysis: Choose the indicator type (Urban Expansion, Damage, Flood, NDVI)
  3. Step 3 — Parameters: Set date range, resolution, priority, data sources, export formats
  4. Step 4 — Review: Review cost estimate (EECU) and submit
Tip: You can also submit jobs via the AI assistant using natural language. Example: "Run urban expansion analysis on Geneva". AI-submitted jobs are fully tracked in Jobs and Job History.

6. How Detection Works

Primary: Dynamic World Embeddings

Google Dynamic World (GOOGLE/DYNAMICWORLD/V1) applies a convolutional neural network (CNN) to every Sentinel-2 scene, producing per-pixel probabilities for 9 land use/land cover classes at 10m resolution.

Why embeddings beat spectral indices:
  • The CNN captures spatial context — road networks, rooftops, urban patterns
  • Uses all Sentinel-2 bands, not just two bands in a ratio
  • Pretrained on millions of global samples, reducing regional bias
  • Provides probabilistic output for confidence-weighted analysis

Cloud Removal Pipeline

Two-stage approach ensures cloudless imagery:

  1. Scene filtering — Exclude images with >30% cloud cover
  2. Pixel masking — Join S2_CLOUD_PROBABILITY (s2cloudless neural network), mask pixels with probability >40%, then compute temporal median on clear pixels only

Gap Filling

For years with incomplete coverage (especially the current year), a 2-pass approach is used:

  1. Primary pass: Mean composite from the target year
  2. Gap-fill pass: Extend temporal window by 12+ months and use mosaic (most recent pixel) to fill any remaining null pixels

This eliminates detection holes from cloud gaps or sparse acquisition.

Validation: NDBI Cross-Check

The Normalised Difference Built-up Index provides a physics-based secondary check:

NDBI = (SWIR − NIR) / (SWIR + NIR) = (B11 − B8) / (B11 + B8)

7. FAQ & Troubleshooting

Why do layers take a few seconds to load?

Each layer is computed on-the-fly by Google Earth Engine. The first request involves image retrieval, cloud masking, and compositing. GEE caches subsequent requests.

Can I see imagery from a specific date?

Map layers show 6-month median composites for maximum coverage. For date-specific analysis, create a job with your desired date range.

Why does the expansion chart show different values for different AOIs?

Urban area is computed per-AOI. Smaller AOIs (e.g. Usaquén at 65 km²) will naturally show less total urban area than the full Bogotá Metropolitan Area at 1,587 km².

How accurate is the cost estimate?

Estimates are heuristic-based (EECU coefficients × area × resolution). Actual costs may vary ±30%.

The sidebar toggle disappeared

The toggle (≡) is positioned to the right of the UNOPS logo. If the sidebar is collapsed, it appears as a small button at the top.
